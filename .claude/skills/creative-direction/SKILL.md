# Creative direction

Execution is not the problem; **ideas** are. A technically perfect piece that any template could have produced is a
failure. Your job is to find the version of this piece that only *this* material, *these* people and *this* moment
could make — then execute it precisely.

Read `taste-profile` (skill) first if it exists for the user. Then the playbook for your discipline in this folder:
`edit.md` (film/edit), `graphics.md` (logo, type, motion graphics), `thumbnails.md` (thumbnails, posters, covers),
`music.md` (score, sound design), `photo.md` (stills, grading). Producers read all of them.

## The process (do it, and write it down)

Write the result as a short **Creative intent** block (in the brief/storyboard for producers, at the top of your
handoff section for specialists) *before* you build. If you can't fill it in, you don't have an idea yet.

1. **Find the truth.** One sentence: what is this *really* about, emotionally? Not "a couple photoshoot" but "two
   people who can't stop making each other laugh while a stranger tries to make them look serious." Look in the
   material for evidence: the specific gestures, sounds, looks, accidents, jokes. The best ideas are *found*, not
   imposed.
2. **Go wide — 5 concepts, genuinely different.** For this user, start from the **story spines** in `taste-profile`
   (clock/light, condition arc, roll call + one chapter per person, countdown + "coming up", place chapters, material
   spine, then-and-now, the photoshoot pose loop) and the house style — intimate real-life storytelling, creativity
   through structure, light, sound and caption voice, not effects. At least 3 of your 5 concepts should be spines
   that fit the material; the others can push further. Use different angles, not variations:
   - *Point of view* — whose eyes? (the camera itself, the photographer, her, him, the crew, the empty studio)
   - *Structure trick* — countdown, chapters, real time, reverse, before/after, a list, a game, a single take feel
   - *Metaphor / world* — borrow the language of something else (a heist, a nature documentary, a sports broadcast,
     a recipe, a film set, a darkroom)
   - *Tone flip* — play it against expectation (deadpan, epic, mock-serious, tender in a funny piece)
   - *Character* — build it around one person's quirk or one running joke
   For each: one-line idea · the signature move you'd see · why it fits the truth · the risk.
3. **Kill the obvious.** Write down the first idea anyone would have (and the generic execution: centred title,
   HUD brackets, warm grade, uplifting synth, "THE BEST DAY" text). Reject it unless you can twist it. The second and
   third ideas are usually where the good work is.
4. **Choose** with reasons: true to the material, *surprising yet inevitable* (the viewer couldn't predict it but
   it feels right once seen), clear in 3 seconds, ownable (not a stock look), doable in our apps.
5. **One signature device.** Every strong piece has one move people remember (a recurring sound, a framing rule, a
   type behaviour, a colour that means something). One. Executed perfectly, used with intent, not sprinkled.
6. **Plant and pay off.** Introduce a motif early (a sound, a line, a gesture, a graphic) and return to it changed at
   the end. Callbacks make a piece feel *designed*.
7. **Specificity beats polish.** Name real details: "the laugh she does into her hand", "the strobe's double pop",
   "his thumbs-up at 0012 0:01". Generic words (beautiful, cinematic, vibes) mean you haven't looked.
8. **Contrast is the engine.** Quiet → loud, close → wide, still → moving, dark → bright, silence → hit, slow →
   fast. Without contrast nothing lands. Plan where the contrasts are.
9. **Constraints are fuel.** Our apps have limits (synth-only audio, 2 MP stills, no display serif in some apps).
   Design *into* them (e.g. build the music from the camera's own sounds; make the softness a lo-fi aesthetic) rather
   than faking around them.
10. **Restraint.** Remove anything that's there "because we can". Fewer, stronger moves. If a label, effect or
    caption explains what the picture already shows, cut it.

## Creative intent block (template)

```
Truth: <one sentence>
Concepts considered: 1 <…> 2 <…> 3 <…> 4 <…> 5 <…>
Rejected obvious: <the first idea and why it's out>
Chosen: <concept> — because <reasons>
Signature device: <the one memorable move>
Motif → payoff: <planted where> → <returns how>
Contrasts: <where the big shifts are>
Risks & how we'll know it works: <…>
```

## Working with the team
- The producer's concept is a direction, not a cage. Specialists must bring **their own idea** inside it (a logo
  that means something, a theme that develops, a cut with a payoff). If the spec forces something generic, propose a
  better version in your report — don't just comply silently.
- Hand ideas forward: put your signature device and motifs in handoff.md so the next app can echo them (the
  composer's motif → the motion designer's timing → the editor's payoff).
- Before you report, run the `creative-review` skill on your own work. If it fails, iterate before delivering.
