# Graphics & motion playbook (graphic-designer, motion-designer)

**This user's direction first** (see `taste-profile`): graphics are quiet and few. What their references actually use:
small serif/humanist title and chapter/place names that fade in on the arrival shot; first-person captions (no box);
a few bold yellow caps labels with an arrow that are jokes and name tags at once; small tracked feature-film credits over
everyday moments ("DIRECTED BY…" over breakfast); a mock-TV identity for one ritual; galleries of the photographer's
stills on white or black between chapters; a quiet black end card. No HUDs, pills, callouts, pops, glitches or
light leaks as decoration. Your creativity goes into **type voice, timing and one meaningful device**, not into more
graphics. Motion = gentle fades and eased drifts, timed to sound and real events.

## Find the visual language *inside* the subject
Don't reach for generic "cinematic" kit (HUD brackets, REC dots, glitch, light leaks, centred tracked caps). Ask:
what objects, marks and rituals belong to this world? For a photoshoot: contact-sheet grease-pencil circles and
crop marks, film edge codes and frame numbers, a light meter needle, the photographer's tablet grid, a shutter blade,
a lens cap, a backdrop roll, polaroid ink developing, a flash's afterimage. Pick **one** and make it the system.

## Logos & wordmarks
- A mark should hold an *idea* (a double meaning, a hidden shape, a gesture), not just a shape. Sketch 10 thumbnails
  fast; most will be obvious — the 7th–10th are where surprise lives.
- Type has a personality: choose (or modify) letters so the word *behaves* like its meaning (a "STILL" that is
  perfectly still while "HOLD" is slightly off-axis; a letter replaced by a meaningful glyph).
- Test: does it work at 32 px, in one colour, animated, and on top of footage? Is it ownable — could it be any brand?

## Layout & type
- Asymmetry and tension beat centred everything. Use a grid and break it once, deliberately.
- Scale contrast: one huge element, the rest small. Avoid everything medium.
- Texture and imperfection (paper grain, ink, slight misregistration) make digital work feel made.

## Motion
- **Motivated motion.** Every move should come from the world: a frame advance, a shutter blink, a strobe pop, a
  focus pull (blur → sharp), paper sliding, ink drying. Not generic slide-ups and wipes.
- One hero gesture per piece; everything else calm. Timing to sound: anticipation 2–4 frames before the hit, settle
  after. Vary easing per element so it doesn't feel like one preset.
- Kinetic type should act out the words (a word that "holds still" while the rest jitters, a countdown that is the
  photographer's fingers).

## Generic tells to avoid
Default slide-up labels · pills on everything · HUD/REC clichés · centred title + small date · identical easing on all
layers · decorative motion with no source · colours applied without meaning.
