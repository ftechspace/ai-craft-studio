# Music & sound playbook (sound-designer)

You can't hear, so creativity lives in the **decisions** — when music exists at all, what switches it on and off, and
the writing.

## This user's references: sound is the main storytelling tool
- **Natural sound first** in every section (rain, room tone, kettles, laughter, talk). Music is **earned or removed**,
  not a percentage: switched **on** by an on-screen action (a record needle, a lamp, a door, a shutter) or once the
  viewer has settled in; switched **off** by a change (nightfall, arrival somewhere, a confession, tears, chaos).
  Silence marks chapter breaks; 3–5 s of near-silence before a big reveal. One loved reference has no music at all.
- Under speech ≈ 12–16 dB below the voice; full level only in event/shoot montages; a cinematic passage rides **one
  arc** (enters on the first strong wide, builds to one peak, fades on arrival somewhere quiet), cut on phrases.
- The references' music is **real and warm**: lo-fi, acoustic guitar/piano, soft strings and pads, gentle percussion.
  SoundCraft has only synths, so: (1) **ask for licensed tracks** (Artlist / Epidemic Sound / YouTube Audio Library)
  and plan where they go; (2) otherwise make *few* short cues and lean on natural sound; (3) build warmth from the
  footage's own sounds (shutter clicks as percussion, room tone as a pad bed, a laugh as a motif).
- Your most valuable deliverable is often a **sound plan**: a cue sheet saying where natural sound carries, where
  music enters and on what action, where it drops out and why, where the silences are.

## When you do write music
- A 4–7-note motif with a recognisable rhythm; state it simply, develop it (new harmony, register, instrument), resolve
  it at the end. Borrowed chords, a pedal, a suspension that resolves on the big moment.
- Warm synth craft: detuned soft pads, plucks with short filter decay + delay, sine sub, no raw bright saw leads;
  humanise velocity (±10–20 %) and timing.
- Arrange in arcs; take something away in the bar before a peak.

## Generic tells to avoid
Wall-to-wall music · four-on-the-floor + saw arpeggio · a 4-chord loop with no motif · every section the same density ·
stingers/whooshes/risers as decoration · music that never stops for the picture.
