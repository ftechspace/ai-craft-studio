# Retention Rules

Viewers decide in seconds whether to stay. Every rule here exists to remove a reason
to leave. Taste from `creative-direction` and `taste-profile` can override the numbers,
never the principles.

## The hook (first 3-15 seconds)

- **Open on the most interesting thing,** not the beginning. Pull the best line,
  result, reveal or conflict to the front ("cold open"), then rewind.
- **Pick one hook type:**
  - Result first - show the finished thing / outcome, then how we got there
  - Question - a question the viewer now needs answered
  - Conflict or stakes - something could go wrong
  - Pattern break - an unexpected image or line
- **Movement in frame 1.** No black, no slow fade-ins, no logo stings, no channel intro
  before the hook.
- **State the promise** by ~15 s: what they get for watching.
- Short-form: the hook must land in the first 1-2 seconds, with on-screen text
  reinforcing it.

## Pacing

- **Keep something changing — but cut by moments, not a timer** (`moment-editing`). Typical on long-form: a new
  visual every few seconds in talking-head A-roll; short-form is faster. A change can be a cut,
  a punch-in (110-120% scale), B-roll, a graphic, a text pop, or a camera angle change.
- **Punch-ins hide jump cuts.** Alternate between normal and punched-in framing across
  jump cuts in talking-head footage.
- **Cut on the breath, not the word.** Keep natural rhythm; don't strip every pause or
  speech becomes robotic.
- **Vary rhythm.** Fast stretches need a short slow moment after them, or the edit
  becomes noise. Slow down for emotion and important information.
- **Cut the middle hard.** Retention drops most in the middle. If a section doesn't
  move the story forward, cut it or shorten it.

## Pattern interrupts (long-form)

Every 30-90 seconds, break the pattern so attention resets:
- a sound effect or music change
- a change of location, angle or format (talking head → screen recording → B-roll)
- an on-screen graphic, meme, or text callout
- a direct question to the viewer
- an open loop ("I'll show you the result at the end")

## Open loops

Plant questions early and pay them off later. Track each loop in the edit plan and make
sure every loop is closed before the end.

## Audio carries retention

- Bad audio loses viewers faster than bad picture. Voice must be clean and intelligible.
- Music under talking: ducked well below voice; change music at section changes.
- SFX on graphics and transitions (whooshes, pops, risers) - subtle, not cartoonish
  unless that is the style.

## Endings

- **Deliver the payoff, then leave quickly.** No long outro recap.
- The CTA is one sentence. On YouTube, the last 5-20 s can be an end screen pointing to
  the next video - plan footage that leaves room for it.
- Short-form: consider a loopable ending (the last frame flows into the first).

## Repurposing long-form into short-form

Good short clips come from moments that:
- make sense without context
- have a clear hook in the first sentence
- contain one complete idea, story or payoff
- end strongly (punchline, reveal, strong statement)

Reframe to 9:16 by following the subject (speaker's face or the action), not a fixed
centre crop. Re-caption for short-form style. Don't reuse the long-form intro.
