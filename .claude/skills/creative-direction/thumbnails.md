# Thumbnails, posters & covers playbook (photo-artist, layout-designer)

## Thumbnails (YouTube) — this user's default
Their references (MAZELEE's top 30 by views, the cozy and Japan vlogs) show **no text, arrows, circles or emoji on the
thumbnail**. The thumbnail is **a real, filmic frame of the most emotional moment**; the title does the verbal work.
- **Find the frame, don't design one.** Scan the footage with dense `contact.exe range` sheets (0.1–0.25 s) around the
  peak moments: a genuine laugh, a tender or quiet face, a real reaction, a look between two people, a small kid doing
  something tiny. Quiet faces win as often as loud ones.
- Faces 20–45 % of the height (intimate) or a clear line-up/silhouette that tells the story in one look (suitcases + a
  family = moving). A wide shot with tiny people fails — pick a closer moment.
- Finish it like a photographer: crop with intent, local light on faces, remove distractions (stands, cables, signs),
  grade soft and warm to match the film (lifted or gently rolled blacks, natural dense skin), sharpen lightly.
- **Write the title with it** (it carries stakes and specifics): first person ("we/our/my"), names the people or
  relationship, one concrete hook (a number, a duration, "#1", "first"), CAPS on 1–3 key words, optional
  `*lowercase aside*`, curiosity that teases feeling and is paid off. Never repeat the title on the image.
- A/B tests: two different *moments* (a quiet one vs a loud one; a look between them vs a look at us), not two layouts.
- Check at 168×94 on white and dark backgrounds: the emotion must read without any text.
- Text/graphic thumbnails only when the brief asks for them; then: ≤ 3–4 words that add what the image doesn't say,
  one focal point, high contrast, timestamp corner clear.

## Posters & covers
- One image, one idea, one line. Let a real moment carry it; type quiet and small (the house caption voice), or a
  restrained title. Steal the *structure* of great poster traditions, not their surface.
- Type and image should interact (overlap, tuck behind, frame), not sit in separate boxes.

## Generic tells to avoid
Text on the thumbnail by default · tiny figures in a wide frame · posed instead of real expressions · glow/stroke to
fix contrast · photo top / title middle / three small pics bottom posters.
