# Vlog Editing Techniques

Use these as tools, not decoration. The creator's taste profile decides how much of
each to use: a calm creator gets fewer effects, an energetic one gets more.

## Talking to camera (A-roll)
- **Jump cuts + punch-ins:** alternate 100% and ~115% scale across jump cuts so cuts
  feel intentional. Don't punch in on every cut - use it for emphasis.
- **Cutaways:** cover jump cuts and long talking with B-roll of what is being described.
- **Keep the breath:** remove dead air, but not every pause. Pauses before a punchline
  or an emotional line are part of the performance.
- **Reactions are gold:** a laugh, a face, a silence often says more than the line.

## B-roll sequences
- **Shot progression:** establishing (where are we) → wide → medium → detail
  (hands, food, textures) → reaction (faces). This makes a place feel real.
- **Shot length is an outcome, not a target:** each shot lasts as long as its moment (`moment-editing`). In a
  deliberate montage the moments are short beats (a step, a splash, a glance); calm, beautiful moments are held until
  they land.
- **Cut on action:** cut during movement (a door opening, a step, a turn) so cuts flow.
- **Cut to the beat** for music-driven sequences - on strong beats, not every beat.
- **Motivated B-roll:** each cutaway shows what's being said or felt, never random.

## Transitions between places and times
- **Match cut:** similar shape, movement or colour from one place to the next.
- **Whip pan / movement transition:** cut between two fast camera movements in the
  same direction.
- **Walk-through / object wipe:** someone or something passes the lens; cut in the dark frame.
- **Sound bridge (J-cut):** next scene's sound starts before its picture. The most
  useful vlog transition - it pulls the viewer forward.
- **L-cut:** current audio continues over the next shot - smooth for conversations.
- **Location card / title:** simple text to say where and when, in the creator's style.
- Hard cuts are fine. Fancy transitions only when they add meaning or energy.

## Time
- **Timelapse / speed ramps** for travel, setting up, cooking, sunsets, crowds.
- **Montage** to skip routine (getting ready, driving, waiting) in seconds.
- **Slow motion** only for moments that deserve weight (a reveal, emotion, beauty)
  and only on footage shot at high frame rate.
- **Time jumps:** text ("3 hours later") or a sound cue to signal skipped time.

## Sound design
- **Ambient sound under B-roll** - market noise, traffic, wind, birds, crowd - makes the
  place real. Never drop B-roll audio to silence without reason.
- **Music changes with the story:** new section, new mood, new track or variation.
- **Drop the music** for important lines or emotional moments - silence hits hard.
- **Small SFX:** whooshes on fast transitions, pops on text, risers into reveals -
  only if the style calls for them.
- **Ducking:** music always below the voice when the creator speaks.

## Text and captions
- Location and time cards, names of people/places, short jokes or comments as text.
- Use text to add a second layer (a funny aside, honest commentary) - classic vlog humour.
- Keep a consistent font and style from `creative-direction`.

## Look and continuity
- Match colour between cameras and phones so the day feels like one story.
- Keep a consistent look across vlogs - viewers recognize a channel by its look.
- Warmer/brighter for happy, cooler/darker for tense or reflective sections - subtle.

## Emotional pacing map
Plan the energy across the vlog like a wave, not a flat line:
hook (high) → setup (medium) → rising moments → a calm breathing moment →
peak / turn → resolution (calm, warm) → short outro.
Two high-energy sections in a row need a calm moment between them.
