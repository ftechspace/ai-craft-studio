# Genre-Specific Editing Playbooks for a Freelance Editor (for an AI film-editor agent)

Scope note for the report writer: sources for this topic are uneven. Platform guidance (Google/YouTube ABCDs, TikTok Ads help) is primary and current; most genre craft advice comes from practitioner blogs (No Film School, PremiumBeat, RoughCut, Artlist) and vendor blogs. Exact beat timings are rarely published as rules. Where a playbook below gives beat-by-beat timings that are not directly in a source, they are in the **Inferences** section and are my synthesis (scaled from the sourced structures), not cited fact. Useful tempo math used throughout: one 4/4 bar = 240 / BPM seconds; one beat = 60 / BPM seconds.

---

## 1. Behind-the-scenes (BTS) of a photoshoot / fashion / studio shoot

### Takeaway
A photo-shoot BTS is a short (about 1–4 min) music-driven montage of process (hair/make-up, wardrobe, gear, photographer at work, interaction with the model) that pays off with the finished stills, usually at the end or as "shot -> result" pairs. The biggest failure is a boring, process-heavy cut; the "flash moment" reveal technique is common practice but I found no authoritative written source for it.

### Cited Findings
- Ideal BTS length for a photography business video is "usually from one to four minutes." — [Nikon USA](https://www.nikonusa.com/en/learn-and-explore/a/tips-and-techniques/how-to-create-a-behind-the-scenes-video-for-your-photography-business.html)
- Include the final retouched stills; the author "usually ha[s] the editor put the images in at the end of the video." Give the editor the retouched finals, the music and the target length before editing starts. — [Nikon USA](https://www.nikonusa.com/en/learn-and-explore/a/tips-and-techniques/how-to-create-a-behind-the-scenes-video-for-your-photography-business.html)
- Shot coverage to look for: a variety "from wide to close-up"; hair and make-up, wardrobe, gear, the photographer at work, interactions with models; a tripod time-lapse of the set build started before setup begins. Editor should "choose clips with the most emotion" and clips "that best express your personality." — [Nikon USA](https://www.nikonusa.com/en/learn-and-explore/a/tips-and-techniques/how-to-create-a-behind-the-scenes-video-for-your-photography-business.html)
- Sound: licensed music sets the tone; voice-over or an on-set interview (photographer describing the vision, or the client) is optional but "adds depth." Include a logo and optionally team credits. — [Nikon USA](https://www.nikonusa.com/en/learn-and-explore/a/tips-and-techniques/how-to-create-a-behind-the-scenes-video-for-your-photography-business.html)
- Common mistakes: "The worst thing you can do is show a video of your shoot that looks boring"; unlicensed music; too little footage; over-long cuts. — [Nikon USA](https://www.nikonusa.com/en/learn-and-explore/a/tips-and-techniques/how-to-create-a-behind-the-scenes-video-for-your-photography-business.html)
- "Shot vs result" format exists in published work: a fashion BTS made of point-of-view clips, each followed by the finished retouched photo. — [PetaPixel (Gerd Tschebular POV BTS)](https://petapixel.com/2015/06/12/bts-a-point-of-view-look-at-a-fashion-shoot-by-gerd-tschebular/)
- When BTS is posted to Reels/TikTok it is subject to the short-form rules in section 9 (9:16, hook in first 3 s, safe zones). — [TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)

### Inferences
- **Durations to offer:** social cut 15–30 s (9:16), standard BTS 60–90 s, portfolio BTS 2–3 min (inside Nikon's 1–4 min ceiling).
- **Beat structure (60–90 s cut):**
  - 0:00–0:03 Hook: the strongest strobe-pop/flash frame or a fast "shot -> final image" pair, so the viewer sees the payoff first (applies the section 9 hook rule).
  - 0:03–0:15 Setup: set build time-lapse, gear close-ups (lens, light modifiers, tethered screen), arrival.
  - 0:15–0:35 Prep: hair/make-up macro shots, wardrobe steaming/pinning, styling details; cut on the music's bars.
  - 0:35–1:05 Shooting block: photographer at work (over-shoulder through viewfinder, wide of set with strobes firing, model posing, monitor/tether review, team reactions). Insert 2–4 "flash moments": cut on the strobe-fire frame, hold or freeze 6–12 frames, then cut or crossfade to the matching final still (camera-shutter SFX on the cut).
  - 1:05–1:20 Payoff: 3–6 finished stills, each 1–2 bars, optionally the BTS frame side-by-side or wiped to the still.
  - Last 2–4 s: logo/credits (photographer, model, MUA, stylist).
- **Shot list to search the footage for:** wide establishing of studio/location; strobe firing (look for frames with a sudden luminance spike, a strong automatic detection cue); photographer's face/hands on camera; POV through viewfinder or tethered monitor; model mid-pose and laughing between frames; MUA/stylist details; team reactions to the monitor; set build time-lapse.
- **Pacing:** upbeat track, ~100–128 BPM for fashion/studio (bar = ~1.9–2.4 s); prep shots 1 bar, hero moments 2 bars. Slow motion (if 50/60p+ was shot) for hair flips, fabric, and the moment of a pose; keep the flash pop at real speed so it reads as a "pop."
- **Colour:** grade BTS video slightly more natural than the finals so the finished stills feel like the "upgrade"; match white balance across cameras (strobe light ~5400K is cited for stills in general, see [Neil van Niekerk tangents](https://neilvn.com/tangents/tag/flash-photography-tips/) search snippet; treat as approximate).
- **Common mistakes (inferred):** showing the finals only at the very end on a long cut (viewers drop before the payoff); including dead setup time; letting a single flash frame cause a strobing/epilepsy risk in fast repetition.

### Gaps
- No authoritative written source found for the specific "flash-sync SFX + freeze frame -> final photo" technique; it is common creator practice but unsourced here.
- No published data on ideal ASL or music tempo for BTS edits.

## 2. Wedding films, pre-wedding/engagement films, couple and portrait sessions

### Takeaway
The wedding highlight is audio-led: pick the spoken story (vows, speeches, letters) first, build a five-act arc (opening -> build -> ceremony climax -> resolution -> closing line), then fit music and visuals. Keep it lean (8–15 soundbites for ~7 min; fewer for a 3–5 min highlight), don't follow the day chronologically, and deliver several cuts (full film, highlight, teaser, 9:16 social clips).

### Cited Findings
- Five-act structure for a 7-min highlight: **Opening 0:00–1:00** (one universal line from a parent/officiant over slow atmospheric B-roll; don't open with vows, a joke or a long speech; "short, evocative, and leave the audience wanting more"); **Build 1:00–3:00** (speeches telling the love story, 2–3 speakers, "let each moment breathe"); **Ceremony/Climax 3:00–5:00** (most personal vows, officiant words, rings, first kiss; music peaks here; don't cut vows with speech audio); **Resolution 5:00–6:30** (recessional, first moments married, parent reaction, humour; don't match climax energy); **Closing 6:30–7:00** (one strong 1–2 sentence line, couple leaving, fade music to black). — [RoughCut (Apr 2026)](https://roughcut.com/blog/how-to-structure-wedding-highlight-film)
- "8–15 soundbites, not 30" for a 7-min film; shortlist 2–3 candidates per section and pick on emotional delivery and audio cleanliness; transcribe everything first; cut test: "If you can remove a clip and the story still works, remove it." Sync music last (choose words first, then adjust trims to music). Mistakes: too many soundbites, rushing vows, cutting vows with speech audio, ending on logistics or a trailing clip. — [RoughCut](https://roughcut.com/blog/how-to-structure-wedding-highlight-film)
- Contrasting view on the opening: open the highlight with a speech or the vows to introduce the couple; save full ceremony and speeches for the long film; include a vow snippet, cake cutting and first dance in the highlight. — [PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/5-tips-editing-cinematic-wedding-highlight-videos) (contradicts RoughCut's "don't open with vows")
- Don't follow chronological order of the day; weave clips for the most engaging story. — [PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/5-tips-editing-cinematic-wedding-highlight-videos)
- Music: often two songs, a slow, steady track for intro/ceremony and a faster track for reception; choose to suit the couple (listen to the DJ's selections); license everything, since takedowns/royalty claims hit the videographer. — [PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/5-tips-editing-cinematic-wedding-highlight-videos)
- Transitions: pan off a subject and pan the same direction into the next, cross-dissolve; slider moves past foreground to reveal; fade to black by moving into a dark object or a guest passing the lens; light leaks sparingly (mainly reception); hard cuts and cross-cutting in getting-ready; J-cuts and L-cuts in the ceremony. — [PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/5-tips-editing-cinematic-wedding-highlight-videos)
- Colour: correct white balance and match multiple cameras (mismatches are common because weddings move fast); grade in a dedicated tool. Keep the highlight well under 10 min. — [PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/5-tips-editing-cinematic-wedding-highlight-videos)
- Opening suggestion 0:00–0:30 built from rings, a candid getting-ready moment or a wide venue shot; don't open with the processional. — [dev.to / FirstCut Studio](https://dev.to/firstcutstudio/how-to-make-a-wedding-highlight-video-without-a-videographer-28a3)
- Course curriculum for a highlight covers crafting a hook, music transitions, "nontraditional endings", and flow from first look to open dance; prep the long-form/feature timeline (ceremony, speeches, multicam) before cutting the highlight. — [Udemy: Edit Cinematic Wedding Films](https://www.udemy.com/course/edit-cinematic-wedding-films/)
- Teaser length is not standardised: a 60–90 s "cinematic teaser for save-the-date & social reveals" — [The Wedding Cut](https://theweddingcut.lovable.app/); 15–30 s teaser clips — [CapCut template site](https://capcuttemplate.it.com/?p=200); 1–2 min trailer for Instagram/YouTube — [Fiverr listing](https://fiverr.com/saimrabbani123/edit-a-cinematic-wedding-trailer). All vendor sources.
- For Instagram, one wedding-marketing guide recommends several separate 9:16 videos (e.g., 4) with clips no longer than 10–15 s rather than one long teaser. — [Maroo](https://www.maroo.us/blogs/6-tips-to-using-tiktok-reels-for-wedding-pros)
- Stillmotion (wedding/doc filmmakers) frame story via the MUSE process: four pillars People, Place, Purpose, Plot, built in a "listen" then "construct" phase; "we must speak to the heart to move the mind." — [ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/muse-a-storytelling-tool/); [Brightcove](https://www.brightcove.com/?p=1316); [Wipster](https://www.wipster.io/blog/storytelling-with-heart)

### Inferences
- **Highlight film 3–5 min (scale RoughCut's 7-min arc to 4:00):**
  - 0:00–0:30 Opening: atmospheric B-roll (venue wide, rings/details, dress hanging, light through windows) under one evocative line (parent, officiant, or a letter read aloud). Slow motion welcome.
  - 0:30–1:30 Build: getting ready (cross-cut bride/groom), first look/reveal; 1–2 speech lines that tell *how they met / who they are*.
  - 1:30–2:45 Climax: ceremony, processional reaction, vows (let 1–2 vow lines play uninterrupted), rings, kiss; music swells/peaks.
  - 2:45–3:40 Resolution: recessional, couple portraits (golden hour), entrances, humour line, dance floor energy (switch to second, faster song if used).
  - 3:40–4:00 Closing: one strong final line, sparkler/exit or a quiet couple moment, music resolves, fade to black, monogram/title.
- **Teaser/trailer 60–90 s:** 0–5 s cold open (striking image or a line of audio); 5–30 s build on a slow track with 1–2 voice lines; 30–60 s rise to the strongest vow/speech line on the music's lift; 60–80 s fast montage of party energy; final 5–10 s title card (names, date, location).
- **Social cut 30–60 s (9:16):** hook frame in the first 1–2 s (kiss, reveal, tears, confetti); a single voice line; cut on bars; title in safe zone (see section 9); end on a loopable frame.
- **Pre-wedding/engagement and couple/portrait sessions:** usually 1–3 min, music-led (no vows), often with a short voice-over (couple answering "how we met") or none; structure = establish place -> playful moments -> intimate close-ups -> golden-hour climax -> quiet end. Lean heavily on slow motion (hair, walking, twirls, hands) and natural sound bites (laughter).
- **Shot list:** details (rings, invitations, shoes, perfume), getting-ready candids, first look reactions, processional (both directions: walking + partner's reaction), vows close-ups, ring exchange insert, kiss, recessional, family hugs, speeches with audience reaction cutaways, cake, first dance (wide + close), party, sparkler/exit, couple portraits at golden hour.
- **Pacing:** opening shots 3–6 s, mid build 2–4 s, party montage 0.5–1.5 s (bar-synced). Slow motion reserved for emotion/beauty (walks, veil, twirls, confetti), not for speech moments (lip sync must be real speed).
- **Colour:** soft, warm, slightly lifted blacks/"film" look is a common convention; skin tones are the priority. (Convention claim, not sourced.)

### Gaps
- No authoritative published standard for teaser/social-cut lengths (only vendor listings); no source found for Joe Simon, Parker Walbeck or Matt Johnson specific frameworks within the tool budget.
- No sourced data on slow-motion frame rates/ratios for wedding work.

## 3. Documentary and short doc / mini-doc

### Takeaway
Build the story from the interviews first (A-roll radio cut), using a simple three-act / hook-information-closure arc, then cover it with B-roll that illustrates, hides jump cuts and adds rhythm; story frameworks (MUSE: People, Place, Purpose, Plot) help choose what the film is about before cutting.

### Cited Findings
- Start with a rough assembly using only interviews, no B-roll; ignore visuals while building structure; test whether the story works as audio alone, like a radio/podcast piece; use a basic three-act structure; duplicate and version sequences at each stage. — [No Film School, "How to Cut Short Documentary Films, Part Two" (Ryland Brooks)](https://nofilmschool.com/2015/11/how-cut-short-documentary-films-part-two-building-story-adding-b-roll)
- A documentary is "a lot like an essay or article": hook the viewer at the beginning, present new information, provide closure at the end; schedule time after each interview to shoot B-roll. — [Indiana University documentary tips](https://jkmedia.sitehost.iu.edu/articles/interview_tips.shtml)
- B-roll illustrates points, hides jump cuts from removed stumbles/pauses, and breaks up talking heads ("a stationary person talking into the camera can get pretty boring"). — [Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/discover/b-roll)
- Common pattern: start on A-roll to establish the subject, then go to B-roll illustrating what they say while their voice continues; juxtaposition (contrasting imagery against what is said) creates point of view. — search results summarising [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2015/11/how-cut-short-documentary-films-part-two-building-story-adding-b-roll) and [QuickFrame](https://quickframe.com/blog/a-roll-vs-b-roll) (attribution from search summary)
- Stillmotion's MUSE: an 8-step process in two phases (listen, construct) with four pillars (People, Place, Purpose, Plot); place is chosen for relevance to the character and story. — [ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/muse-a-storytelling-tool/); [No Film School excerpt](https://nofilmschool.com/2015/06/excerpt-from-muse-comprehensive-process-impactful-storytelling)
- Micro-documentaries for short-form must be narrow (one problem, one transformation); texture comes from unscripted speech, real subjects with backstory, natural environments, ambient sound, and visuals showing effort or imperfection. — [CapCut micro-documentary guide](https://www.capcut.com/create/micro-documentary-short-form-video)

### Inferences
- **Durations:** social mini-doc 60–180 s; web short doc 3–8 min; festival short doc 10–20 min.
- **Beat structure (5-min mini-doc):**
  - 0:00–0:20 Cold open: the most arresting line or moment (a verité scene or a striking quote) + strong image.
  - 0:20–0:45 Title + world: establishing B-roll of place, natural sound up.
  - 0:45–1:45 Act 1 (who/what): character introduced in their own words; what they want.
  - 1:45–3:30 Act 2 (obstacle/journey): the problem, stakes, process; alternate interview with verité and "breathing" moments (music-only or nat-sound-only, 5–15 s) every ~60–90 s.
  - 3:30–4:30 Act 3 (turn/resolution): change, insight, payoff scene.
  - 4:30–5:00 Closing line + final image, end card/credits.
- **Shot list:** interview A-cam (medium) + B-cam (tight/profile); hands at work; establishing wides of place; verité scenes of the subject doing the thing; detail inserts (objects, documents, photos); archival; transitional nat-sound moments (doors, footsteps, traffic).
- **Pacing:** slower than ads/social; B-roll shots 3–6 s, holds on faces after an emotional line (let the pause play). Remove "ums" but keep meaningful pauses.
- **Music:** sparse, ambient/piano/strings; drop music out under the most important lines; let nat sound carry transitions (J/L cuts).
- **Colour:** naturalistic; consistent skin tones across interview setups.
- **Mistakes:** wall-to-wall talking heads; B-roll that is literal ("says car, show car") rather than meaningful; music under every second; burying the hook.

### Gaps
- I did not reach primary interviews with ACE documentary editors or Gael Chandler's *Cut by Cut* within the tool budget; no published ASL norms for documentary were found.

## 4. Music videos (performance vs narrative, cutting to the beat, energy mapping)

### Takeaway
Map the song's structure (intro/verse/pre/chorus/bridge/outro, drops) first, lay a performance backbone, then layer narrative/B-roll; cut on bars or beats but vary shot lengths so cuts don't become predictable, using fast cuts for high-energy sections and longer takes for emotional ones. Research shows music-video shot durations often equal one bar of the song's tempo.

### Cited Findings
- Three structural approaches: performance-led, narrative-led, or hybrid (performance anchors the edit; other shots add rhythm and scale). — [Hedra docs](https://www.hedra.com/docs/pages/app/content-creation/creating-music-videos)
- Place markers on beats (tap along in Premiere) for cut points; use fast cuts for energy and longer takes for emotional, quieter moments; grade and add effects after picture lock; use proxies for 4K. — [Pixflow](https://pixflow.net/blog/how-to-edit-a-music-video-in-premiere-pro/)
- Don't make every shot the same length: equal-length clips let viewers anticipate cuts; vary lengths and cut on secondary beats, not only at the downbeat of each bar. — [No Film School / Larry Jordan](https://nofilmschool.com/2014/09/larry-jordan-teaches-us-how-create-video-montage-set-music)
- Default to cutting on the beat in 4/4 with occasional two-beat or two-bar cuts to avoid monotony. — [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4905278)
- Cut the performance first as the framework, pinpoint drops and choruses, then add B-roll; mirror the song form (verse, repeated chorus, bridge); alternate wides and close-ups; don't cut on every single beat (overwhelms and disconnects viewer from song). — search summary of [No Film School "5 Tips for Speeding Up Your Music Video Edit"](https://nofilmschool.com/speed-up-music-video-edit) and [Wedio Academy](https://academy.wedio.com/how-to-edit-a-music-video) (exact attribution between these two not confirmed)
- Academic analysis: the most common shot durations in music videos often line up with the tempo, e.g., dominant shot length ~2.72 s for an 88 BPM song and ~1.93 s for a 124 BPM song (both = one 4/4 bar); about one fifth of clips in the dataset showed bar-level synchronisation. — [arXiv 2108.00970, "Is there a language of music-video clips?"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.00970)
- A 40-video study found average shot length differs by music-video category (values not retrieved). — [Revista Latina de Comunicación Social](https://nuevaepoca.revistalatinacs.org/index.php/revista/article/view/807)

### Inferences
- **Energy map procedure:** detect BPM and downbeats; label sections; assign an energy score (1–5) per section; set target cut interval per section: intro/verse = 2 bars (480/BPM s) or 1 bar; pre-chorus = 1 bar tightening to half-bar; chorus = half-bar to beat (60/BPM s) with accents on snare hits; bridge = break the pattern (long take, slow motion, or a narrative scene); final chorus = densest cutting + biggest shots; outro = long hold.
- **Duration:** match the song (typically 2:30–4:00); social teasers 15–30 s built around the hook/chorus.
- **Shot list:** performance passes (wide, medium, tight, profile, moving), lip-sync close-ups, instrument close-ups (hands on strings/keys/drums), narrative scenes, abstract/texture B-roll, crowd/dancers, light flares and practicals for transitions.
- **Rules:** lip-sync must be frame-accurate (sync to the playback track); first performance close-up within the first chorus at the latest; save the best/most expensive shot for the final chorus.
- **Slow motion:** shot at high frame rate with playback sped up on set (a known MV technique) yields slow-motion with correct lip sync; in edit, use slow motion for bridges/breakdowns. (Technique is common knowledge; not sourced here.)
- **Colour:** stylised per artist/genre (high-contrast, saturated for pop/hip-hop; desaturated/film emulation for indie). Not sourced.
- **Mistakes:** metronomic cutting on every beat; ignoring song sections; lip-sync drift; burying the artist's face.

### Gaps
- No primary interviews with music-video editors retrieved; ASL values per genre not retrieved from the Revista Latina study.

## 5. Commercials / ads and product videos (6 s, 15 s, 30 s; hook-problem-solution-CTA)

### Takeaway
Follow Google's ABCD framework (Attention, Branding, Connection, Direction): open on a strong hook immediately, brand early and often (with audio mention), feature people and one focused message, and close with a clear, voiced CTA. 6 s bumpers carry one message with a strong opening image and roughly 2 s for the closing thought; 15 s and 30 s build in a problem-to-solution turn.

### Cited Findings
- ABCDs: **Attention** "Get to the heart of the story faster, and use engaging pacing and tight framing"; reinforce message with audio and supers, avoid competing elements; bright, high-contrast visuals. **Branding** "Introduce your brand or product from the start and maintain that presence"; "Audio brand mentions enhance onscreen brand visuals' performance." **Connection** feature people; keep messaging focused and simple; humour, surprise, intrigue. **Direction** add a CTA for a specific objective; "Reinforce your onscreen CTA with voice-over." Google/Kantar: up to 30% lift in short-term sales likelihood, 17% lift in long-term brand contribution (Apr 2021, vendor research). — [Google Ads Help: ABCDs](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551)
- ABCDs by objective: Awareness (audio to grab attention, brand front and centre); Consideration (hero the product, show how it works, plant urgency); Action ("make the product the ad", present the ask after context is set); Full funnel (hook with audio, CTAs throughout getting more direct). Shorts: "Announce yourself with audio", tight framing, let talent "get real". — [Google Ads Help: ABCDs](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551)
- Grab attention within the first 5 s (skip button); ~95% of YouTube ads are watched with sound on. — [Jumpfly](https://www.jumpfly.com/blog/youtube-ads-the-abcds-of-effective-video-ad-creative/) (agency, figure not verified with primary)
- 6 s bumpers: one memorable message; split multi-benefit products into separate bumpers — [AdEspresso](https://adespresso.com/?p=67419); [The HOTH](https://www.thehoth.com/blog/bumper-ads/). Effective bumpers often start with a striking image; give the final thought/CTA about 2 s; trimming a 30 s spot tends to cram the close. — Think with Google bumper editing article (now redirects; summary via search: [Think with Google](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/intl/en-gb/marketing-strategies/video/youtube-bumper-ads-best-practices-video-editing)). Bumpers suit reach/awareness; a Think with Google study of 122 US campaigns reportedly found 70% drove significant awareness lift averaging 9% (2017). — [MegaDigital summary](https://megadigital.ai/en/blog/youtube-bumper-ads/)
- 30 s shape: mood-setting setup, product story in the middle, sign-off; the longer runtime lets the problem-to-solution turn "feel earned, so let each beat breathe instead of packing in cuts"; build one concept and recut to 15 s, 6 s and vertical/square while holding one look. — [Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/videos/30-second-commercial-videos) (vendor)
- TikTok ads: hook in first 6 s; core proposition in first 3 s; structure = hook -> unique selling points -> clear CTA; feature creators/employees/customers; DIY, not overly polished; use sound/music; text 5–10 words per second; transitions, stickers and graphics to retain view time; 9:16, at least 720p, keep within safe zone. — [TikTok Ads Help: Creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)

### Inferences
- **6 s bumper:** 0–1 s striking image + brand/product visible; 1–4 s the single message (one benefit, one action, ~1–3 shots); 4–6 s logo/product + CTA super (about 2 s).
- **15 s:** 0–2 s hook (pattern interrupt, product in action, or a problem shown); 2–5 s problem/desire; 5–11 s solution/product demo (2–4 shots, product hero close-up); 11–15 s brand + CTA (voiced + supered). Brand/product visible by ~2–5 s.
- **30 s:** 0–3 s hook; 3–10 s problem/tension (relatable person); 10–22 s solution/demo/benefits (product hero shots, social proof); 22–27 s emotional payoff/transformation; 27–30 s logo, tagline, CTA. Brand present early (ABCD).
- **Product video shot list:** hero product on clean background (slow push/orbit), macro texture details, product in hand/in use, before/after, packaging/unboxing, lifestyle with real people, UI/screen capture for apps, logo end card.
- **Pacing:** social ads 0.5–2 s per shot; TV-style 30 s 1.5–3 s per shot; slow motion for liquid pours, splashes, fabric, food.
- **Sound:** VO or creator talking to camera (TikTok/Shorts style), sonic logo/brand mention, SFX on product actions (clicks, pours, whooshes).
- **Colour:** brand-accurate product colour is non-negotiable; bright, high-contrast (per ABCD).
- **Mistakes:** brand only at the end; multiple messages; cutdowns made by trimming instead of re-editing; CTA too short to read; text in platform UI zones.

### Gaps
- No primary Meta Business Help Center page retrieved (only vendor summaries); no authoritative 15 s structure from an agency.

## 6. Fashion films (mood, rhythm, styling)

### Takeaway
Fashion films are mood- and garment-led rather than plot-led: choose a temp sound direction first, let the collection's character set the tempo, give the garment's movement (fabric, silhouette, turns, accessories) time on screen, and hold strict colour/styling continuity so several looks feel like one world.

### Cited Findings
- Set a temporary sound direction before editing: it determines how quickly a turn happens, whether a close-up needs more time, and where to break the rhythm; a steady electronic beat makes a walk feel controlled/graphic, sparse ambient sound makes viewers notice fabric and breathing, an irregular beat adds tension. Label temp audio; don't build around unlicensable tracks. — [Cyprus Mail, Seedance fashion case study (Aug 2026)](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/08/10/seedance-2-0-for-fashion-moving-from-mood-board-to-campaign-film)
- Show how the garment behaves (structured coats hold shape, light dresses respond to air, dense knits move slowly); shoes and accessories need their own moments (an accessory may catch light only during a turn); camera relationship changes meaning (distant fixed frame = graphic; close handheld = physical immediacy; slow circular move reveals silhouette). — [Cyprus Mail](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/08/10/seedance-2-0-for-fashion-moving-from-mood-board-to-campaign-film)
- Design for each aspect ratio; "Simply cropping the wide version may remove the gesture or garment detail"; hold continuity rules for colour, light, framing; watch continuity slips (hem length, bag closure, pattern alignment, jewellery side); product must look like the real cut/material. — [Cyprus Mail](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/08/10/seedance-2-0-for-fashion-moving-from-mood-board-to-campaign-film)
- Models' gestures, changing light and the behaviour of materials act as visual markers guiding the sequence. — [designboom / Clideo](https://www.designboom.com/readers/clideo-video-editor-fashion-films-visual-stories/)
- A fashion reel selling a mood can move faster than a tutorial; checkpoint test: if the viewer can't read the caption, understand the product or feel the emotional change, the montage is too fast. — [CapCut montage rhythm guide](https://www.capcut.com/create/music-video-pacing-emotional-montage-rhythm)
- Quick cuts create energy/tension; slow cuts calm/introspection; movement inside the frame adds rhythm without a cut. — [Editing for Film course notes](https://www.hufocw.org/Download/file/30822)

### Inferences
- **Durations:** campaign film 60–120 s; festival fashion film 2–5 min; social cutdowns 6/15/30 s in 9:16.
- **Beat structure (90 s):** 0–10 s atmosphere (location, light, abstract fabric macro); 10–30 s introduce the model/character and first look; 30–70 s rhythm section (look-by-look, walks, turns, detail inserts on beat; break the pattern once with a held close-up or slow motion); 70–85 s peak (strongest look, fastest cutting or most striking image); 85–90 s logo/collection title.
- **Shot list:** full-length walk (towards camera), turns showing silhouette, fabric macros (texture, drape, movement in wind), accessory/shoe details, hands touching fabric, face/beauty close-ups, environment wides, light changes.
- **Slow motion:** heavily used for fabric, hair and turns (fashion convention); keep walks often at real speed for weight.
- **Titles:** minimal, typographic, brand fonts; credits (designer, stylist, MUA, model) at end.
- **Colour:** stylised, consistent world (often desaturated/film-emulation or a bold palette matched to the collection), but garment colour accuracy beats style.
- **Mistakes:** plot-heavy cutting that hides the clothes; equal-length cuts; cropping wide shots for vertical; continuity errors in styling.

### Gaps
- No fashion-film festival (e.g., ASVOFF, Berlin Fashion Film Festival) editing guidelines found; the 1Granary piece ("How to edit a collection like a filmmaker") was not fetched.

## 7. Travel films

### Takeaway
Travel films are music- and sound-design-led montages that ease in slowly, build energy, and ease out; they rely on establishing/detail/movement coverage, matched camera motion and match cuts between locations, and layered ambient sound to create immersion.

### Cited Findings
- Rhythm: "start out slow, gently ease your audience into the experience," build energy and pacing, then ease back down (works for high-energy and introspective travel films). — [No Film School, 5 editing techniques for travel videos](https://nofilmschool.com/editing-techniques-better-travel-videos)
- Use sound effects to strengthen visuals (e.g., layer rain over grey clouds, then cut to the downpour); keep the point of interest in the same area of frame across cuts so viewers don't hunt; don't cut between moving and static shots, or add motion to static shots (crop/keyframe, motion blur, shake). — [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/editing-techniques-better-travel-videos)
- Coverage: establishing shots (wide exteriors/terminals), detail shots (luggage, boarding passes), movement shots (walking, boarding); alternate quick cuts with longer takes; simple transitions aligned to the beat; ambient sound (announcements, footsteps) balanced against music; warm grade = welcoming, cool = subdued. — [Splice (Jun 2026)](https://spliceapp.com/blog/airport-montage-editing-for-travel-creators/)
- Match cuts (shared shape, action or sound) imply continuity between scenes and are used to move a subject/product location to location; J-cuts/L-cuts act as sound bridges. — [Backstage](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/what-is-a-match-cut-75670/)

### Inferences
- **Durations:** social 15–60 s (9:16); cinematic travel film 2–4 min; travel vlog 8–20 min (see section 8).
- **Beat structure (3 min):** 0:00–0:20 slow cold open (sunrise/landscape wide, ambient sound, maybe one VO line); 0:20–0:50 arrival/journey (transport, maps, feet walking, passport/ticket inserts); 0:50–2:10 exploration montage, grouped by place or theme (food, people, landmarks, nature), building tempo with speed ramps/whip-pan transitions on beats; 2:10–2:40 climax (most spectacular location, drone wide, slow motion); 2:40–3:00 wind-down (sunset, reflective close-up, title/location card).
- **Shot list:** drone/aerial wides, landmark establishing, street life, local people (with consent), food macro, hands (touching water/sand, holding coffee), POV walking/driving, transport (train windows, plane wings), time-lapses/hyperlapses, night/neon, texture details.
- **Sound:** layer ambient beds per location (markets, waves, wind, city), SFX on whips/transitions, music with a clear build; drop music for a beat of pure ambience at a turning point.
- **Titles:** location lower-thirds or large place-name typography; optional map animations.
- **Colour:** consistent grade across varied locations/cameras (warm/teal-orange is a common convention; not sourced).
- **Mistakes:** postcard slideshow with no human presence; uniform pacing; music-only with no natural sound.

### Gaps
- No primary source on speed-ramp/hyperlapse conventions or typical travel-film ASL was found.

## 8. Vlogs / YouTube long-form (jump cuts, pacing, B-roll coverage, retention editing)

### Takeaway
Long-form YouTube editing is organised around retention: front-load the payoff and validate the title/thumbnail promise in the first ~30–60 s, move from hype to execution quickly, re-engage on a regular cadence (new information every ~10–15 s, bigger twists every few minutes), and tighten talking heads with jump cuts while covering with B-roll, time-lapse and whip-pan transitions.

### Cited Findings
- MrBeast's leaked internal guide (2024; authenticity confirmed to press by former staff): tease best moments in the first 60 s to limit drop-off; add a twist/escalation roughly every three minutes ("retention editing"). — [OMR](https://omr.com/de/daily/mrbeast-doc-pdf-leak); authenticity: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/09/26/youtube-mrbeast-jimmy-donaldson-leaked-business-handbook-advice), [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/09/17/mrbeast-internal-production-guide-leaked-key-points/)
- Stage model attributed to the guide: minute 0–1 validates the title promise and stakes; minute 1–3 transitions "from hype to action"; minute 3–6 holds the most exciting yet simple content; after that, longer explanations are tolerable; "zero dull moments." — [Alexander Jarvis memo summary](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/); [marketing4ecommerce](https://marketing4ecommerce.net/en/tips-for-youtube-by-mrbeast/) (secondary; exact wording may differ)
- Title/thumbnail set viewer expectations; CTR, AVD and AVP are key metrics. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/09/17/mrbeast-internal-production-guide-leaked-key-points/)
- Mario Joos (ex-MrBeast retention strategist): "It's not the cutting speed, it's the speed at which you introduce progressive novel information"; jokes are non-progressive, so don't stack them (about one per segment); show value within the first 15 s; add new information roughly every 10–15 s; use chapter cues; "I cannot tell you a single video that has 70% retention. That's the biggest myth." — [Vidpros](https://vidpros.com/retention-editing-what-you-can-learn-from-mario-joos/)
- Casey Neistat style: jump cuts in talking heads to consolidate information; cutting off mid-sentence once the audience knows the end of the thought; a story started in one location and finished in another; associative cuts (referential clips); rapid sped-up task montages; whip-pan transitions cut on the blur; time-lapses to skip dead time; mostly straight cuts and little colour work; embraces rawness. — [Artlist](https://artlist.io/blog/editing-storytelling-techniques-casey-neistat/); [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2016/08/7-storytelling-techniques-you-can-learn-filmmaker-youtube-star-casey-neistat)
- Neistat edits chronologically start to finish, then rearranges. — [VloggingPro](https://vloggingpro.com/casey-neistat-guide-to-filmmaking/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- **Durations:** vlog 8–15 min; long-form explainer/challenge 10–25 min.
- **Beat structure (12 min):**
  - 0:00–0:30 Cold open/hook: payoff tease + one-line premise matching title/thumbnail; no logo intro.
  - 0:30–1:00 Stakes/setup: why it matters, what will happen; quick montage preview.
  - 1:00–3:00 Hype -> action: start doing the thing.
  - ~3:00, ~6:00, ~9:00 Re-engagement beats: twist, new challenge, reveal, location change, "but then..." moments.
  - Throughout: new visual or information every ~10–15 s (B-roll, graphic, angle change, SFX, punch-in zoom).
  - Last 60 s: payoff + short outro; avoid signalling the end early ("so that's it" kills retention); end-screen 5–20 s.
- **Talking-head technique:** remove pauses/filler with jump cuts; alternate framing (punch-in 110–130% on alternating cuts) to soften jumps; cover with B-roll every 5–15 s; captions/keyword text for emphasis.
- **B-roll coverage:** every noun/action mentioned should have a visual; time-lapse for transit; screen recordings; maps; whip pans between locations.
- **Music:** low-volume beds that change by section; music switch = scene change; SFX (whoosh, pop, riser) at transitions.
- **Colour:** clean, slightly punchy, consistent skin tones; not a heavy cinematic grade unless channel brand.
- **Mistakes:** slow intros/logo stings; repeated jokes/tangents; cuts that are fast but carry no new information; mismatch between thumbnail promise and content.

### Gaps
- Original MrBeast PDF not read directly; YouTube's own retention benchmarks not retrieved.

## 9. Instagram Reels / TikTok / YouTube Shorts (hooks, loops, on-screen text, trends)

### Takeaway
Vertical 9:16, sound on, hook and core message in the first ~3 s (TikTok asks for proposition in 3 s, hook within 6 s), native/DIY feel with real people, captions at a readable rate, text kept out of the top ~14% and bottom ~35% UI zones, and a clear CTA or a loopable ending. Max lengths are now 3 min on Shorts and Reels, but practitioners still see most strong performers under ~30–60 s.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok: 9:16, at least 720p, within UI safe zone; proposition in first 3 s; hook within first 6 s (suspense, surprise, emotion); hook -> USPs -> CTA; text overlays at 5–10 words per second; use transitions, stickers and graphics to hold view time; use sound/music; DIY, not over-polished; feature creators/employees/customers; lean into trends, memes and challenges. — [TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- YouTube Shorts ABCDs: "Announce yourself with audio", be "unavoidable", tight framing; let talent "get real" and use the product; clear, relevant CTA. — [Google Ads Help: ABCDs](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551)
- YouTube raised Shorts max length from 60 s to 3 min in October 2024. — [Outfy](https://www.outfy.com/blog/how-long-can-youtube-shorts-be) (secondary). YouTube Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie: a Short should be as long as it needs to be and no longer. — [Piktochart](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts.md) (secondary quote)
- Adobe Express survey of 507 creators: under 30 s cited as ideal for views, shares, click-throughs and saves (avg 46% of creators). — [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/youtube-shorts-length-study). Many high-performing Shorts cluster at 15–35 s — [ShortGenius](https://shortgenius.com/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices) (vendor).
- Instagram (Mosseri, Jan 2025, reported secondhand): Reels distribution driven by watch time, sends per reach (DM shares) and likes per reach; watch time measured both relative (% viewed) and absolute (seconds); Reels max raised from 90 s to 3 min; reposted/watermarked content from other platforms gets less reach. — [Stack Influence](https://stackinfluence.com/how-does-the-algorithm-for-reels-work-in-2025/); [Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm/) (secondary, not verified with Instagram's own posts)
- Meta Reels ads (vendor summaries): 9:16 at 1080x1920 master per placement rather than auto-crop; keep text/logos out of top ~14% and bottom ~35%; design to work with and without sound; reported 9:16 Reels ads scored +7 points higher positive response than non-9:16. — [Benly (2026 specs)](https://benly.ai/learn/ad-creative/meta-ads-creative-specs-2026); [Logical Position](https://www.logicalposition.com/blog/supercharge-your-advertising-with-reels) (unverified against Meta primary)
- Loops: some creators keep Shorts ~15 s to let replays do the work. — [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/youtube-shorts-length) (secondary)

### Inferences
- **Lengths:** 7–15 s (loop/trend/aesthetic), 20–35 s (story/reveal/BTS/wedding), 45–90 s (educational/tutorial), up to 3 min only when content earns it.
- **Beat structure (30 s):** 0–1.5 s visual hook (motion in frame 1, face, reveal result first, or a bold text question); 1.5–3 s on-screen promise/proposition; 3–22 s payoff sequence with a new visual every 1–2 s and one mid-point re-hook (~10–15 s); 22–28 s payoff/reveal; 28–30 s CTA or loop bridge (last frame matches/leads into first frame; audio phrase resolves into the start).
- **Loop technique:** end on a frame and sound that flow back into frame 1 (same composition/action, sentence that completes with the opening words).
- **On-screen text:** large, high-contrast, max ~6–8 words per card; centre-upper-middle safe area (for 1080x1920: avoid top ~270 px and bottom ~670 px; also keep clear of the right-side action buttons); burned-in captions for talking content.
- **Trends:** use trending audio when it fits (check commercial licensing for brand accounts), and cut to the audio's beat/drops; use native-feeling text styles.
- **Mistakes:** slow logo intros; 16:9 letterboxed footage; watermarks from other apps; text in UI zones; fading out at the end (kills loop); horizontal-first framing cropped badly.

### Gaps
- No official Instagram/YouTube primary page on loops or safe-zone pixel values retrieved; Mosseri statements are secondhand.

## 10. Corporate / brand stories and event recaps

### Takeaway
Brand stories work best as short customer- or people-centred documentaries (customer as hero, brand as guide, three-act arc) built from interview A-roll and authentic B-roll; event recaps are chronological or thematic highlight montages that set the scene, show the core moments (speakers, reveals, reactions, testimonials) and end on a clear takeaway, with a 2–3 min cinematic version and 30–60 s social cuts.

### Cited Findings
- Decide recap vs teaser first: recaps show the experience/ROI; teasers drive future sign-ups; goals change filming, editing and narrative. — [PlayPlay event recap](https://playplay.com/blog/event-recap-video/); [Loquiz](https://loquiz.com/2022/06/12/how-to-create-corporate-event-highlights-video-that-will-attract-attendees/)
- Structure: open with a scene-setter (venue, arrivals montage); middle carries the core content, told chronologically as the day unfolded; end with a strong takeaway. — [Canon CinemaEOS](https://cinemaeos.canon.ca/?p=3005); [Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/make-a-corporate-event-recap-video)
- Select only the most impactful moments: speeches, product reveals, audience reactions, memorable quotes; testimonials act as social proof; real laughter/reactions make it authentic. — [PlayPlay event highlights](https://playplay.com/blog/event-highlights-video/); [HeyGen](https://www.heygen.com/blog/event-recap-video)
- Cinematic recap uses dramatic transitions, slow-motion sequences, professional grading, chosen music and wide shots showing venue scale; social cut 30–60 s; consistent pacing and lighting make it cohesive; cut multiple reels from one event. — [PlayPlay](https://playplay.com/blog/event-highlights-video/); [Cinebody](https://cinebody.com/cinebody-blog/corporate-event-video-production)
- Brand story: "Your customer is the hero. Not your brand"; pick a hero representative of the buyer persona (e.g., a Health and Safety Officer rather than the CEO). — [Cognitive Films](https://www.cognitivefilms.com/customer-hero-brand-story/)
- Three-act brand film: setup (customer's backstory), confrontation (attempts to solve the problem, realises they need help), resolution (changed, new beliefs about what's possible). — [The Story of Telling](https://thestoryoftelling.com/three-act-structure-brand-stories/)
- Short-form brand docs must be narrow (one problem, one transformation) and use unscripted speech and ambient sound. — [CapCut micro-doc](https://www.capcut.com/create/micro-documentary-short-form-video)

### Inferences
- **Event recap 2–3 min:** 0:00–0:10 hook (biggest moment: keynote applause, reveal, crowd wide) + title; 0:10–0:30 scene-setter (venue exterior, signage, registration, arrivals); 0:30–1:45 core (keynote soundbites as spine, breakouts, networking, product demos, cut by theme or chronology); 1:45–2:20 testimonials (2–3 short attendee/speaker lines); 2:20–2:40 peak (party/awards energy, fastest cutting); 2:40–3:00 takeaway line + logo + next-event date/CTA.
- **Event social cut 30–60 s:** 9:16, hook in 1–2 s, music-led, one soundbite, captions.
- **Brand story 2–3 min:** 0–15 s hook (character in their world + intriguing line); 15–45 s who they are/the problem; 45–120 s the journey and how the brand helped (guide role); 120–160 s transformation/impact; 160–180 s mission line + logo/CTA.
- **Shot list (event):** venue wide/drone, signage/branding, registration, crowd wides, speaker mid + tight + audience reaction, slides/screens, handshakes/networking, product demos, food/details, sponsor logos, testimonials on a clean backdrop, party.
- **Shot list (brand):** interview (CEO/customer/employee), hands-on work, product/service in use, team culture, workplace establishing, customer environment, data/results graphics.
- **Sound:** keynote audio as spine (clean lav/board feed); upbeat corporate/indie-pop music 100–125 BPM for recaps; emotive build for brand stories; nat sound of applause/laughter.
- **Titles:** lower-thirds for speakers (name, title, company) in brand fonts; consistent logo placement.
- **Colour:** clean, bright, brand-safe; match multi-cam footage.
- **Mistakes:** covering everything; long speech excerpts; CEO-as-hero; generic stock music with no build; missing names/titles on speakers.

### Gaps
- No ad-agency or corporate-video-association primary standards found; ASL and duration figures beyond the 30–60 s social cut are practitioner conventions.

## 11. Cross-genre parameters for the agent (pacing, slow motion, music tempo, colour)

### Takeaway
Across genres the same few levers recur: cut rate tied to musical structure and information density, slow motion reserved for emotion/beauty, sound (voice/nat sound) as the narrative spine in story genres and music as the spine in montage genres, and consistent colour across cameras. Published numeric norms are scarce; tempo-derived cut intervals are the most defensible numeric rule.

### Cited Findings
- Music-video dominant shot durations often equal one bar of the song's tempo (2.72 s at 88 BPM; 1.93 s at 124 BPM). — [arXiv 2108.00970](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.00970)
- Vary shot lengths to avoid predictable cuts; cut on secondary beats too. — [No Film School / Larry Jordan](https://nofilmschool.com/2014/09/larry-jordan-teaches-us-how-create-video-montage-set-music)
- Pacing is about the rate of new progressive information, not raw cut speed. — [Vidpros / Mario Joos](https://vidpros.com/retention-editing-what-you-can-learn-from-mario-joos/)
- Quick cuts = energy/tension; slow cuts = calm/introspection; in-frame movement creates rhythm without cuts. — [Editing for Film course notes](https://www.hufocw.org/Download/file/30822)
- Story genres: build the audio/interview spine first (doc radio cut; wedding words-first, music last). — [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2015/11/how-cut-short-documentary-films-part-two-building-story-adding-b-roll); [RoughCut](https://roughcut.com/blog/how-to-structure-wedding-highlight-film)
- Montage genres: choose music (or temp sound direction) before cutting. — [Cyprus Mail](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/08/10/seedance-2-0-for-fashion-moving-from-mood-board-to-campaign-film); Casey Neistat reportedly picks music first — [Artlist](https://artlist.io/blog/editing-storytelling-techniques-casey-neistat/)
- Grade after picture lock; match white balance across cameras. — [Pixflow](https://pixflow.net/blog/how-to-edit-a-music-video-in-premiere-pro/); [PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/5-tips-editing-cinematic-wedding-highlight-videos)

### Inferences
- **Indicative shot-length ranges (synthesis, not sourced norms):** social ads/Reels 0.5–2 s; music video chorus 0.5–1 bar, verse 1–2 bars; fashion 1–4 s with deliberate long holds; wedding highlight 2–5 s (montage 0.5–1.5 s); travel 1–4 s; documentary B-roll 3–6 s; vlog talking-head segments broken every 5–15 s; event recap 1–3 s.
- **Story-spine vs music-spine decision:** if the genre has meaningful speech (doc, wedding highlight, brand story, vlog, event recap) -> build the dialogue spine first; if not (BTS, fashion, travel, music video, most Reels) -> lock music and beat grid first.
- **Slow motion rule:** use when the source was shot at >=50/60 fps and the moment is emotional/beauty/action (fabric, hair, confetti, water, walks, dance); never on speech or lip sync (except MV playback tricks); conform 60p into 24/25p timelines for 2.4–2.5x slow motion.
- **Music tempo by mood (convention):** romantic/emotional 60–90 BPM; documentary/brand reflective 70–100; lifestyle/travel/BTS 95–125; fashion 100–130 or ambient; hype/ad/social 120–150.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or industry-standard ASL tables per commercial genre were found within the tool budget (James Cutting's film ASL research not retrieved); music tempo-by-mood ranges and slow-motion conventions above are unsourced practitioner conventions.
