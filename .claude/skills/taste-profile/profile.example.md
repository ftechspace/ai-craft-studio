## House style (default unless a brief says otherwise)
**Genre:** intimate real-life storytelling — vlog / documentary, not ad or trailer. The people (or, in solo films, the
space and the ritual) are the show. Creativity lives in **structure, light, sound and the caption voice**; effects are
rare and must mean something.

**Pacing**
- Median shot ≈ 4–6 s (references: 3.6–6.2 s), almost nothing under 2 s after the opening; rituals held 8–54 s in a
  few angles; real emotional moments uncut for 15 s – 3 min.
- Two speeds: brisk jump cuts only in talky/funny bits; patience everywhere else.
- Optional fast "promise" montage at the open (≈ 1.3–2 s shots, ≤ 25–30 s), then calm; or open calm on one long held
  frame. Never both fast and long.

**Structure — pick one spine per film** (the spine *is* the idea; write it in the Creative intent):
- *Clock / light* — dawn → lamps → night; grade, practicals and music decay together toward night.
- *Condition* — state it at the open ("It's raining today"), end when it changes ("the rain has stopped").
- *Roll call / one chapter per person* — introduce everyone in ~3 s, then one chapter each, ending on the smallest.
- *Countdown* — name the destination early, number the days/steps, re-promise it ("coming up:") every few minutes.
- *Place chapters* — every place entered the same way: exterior → arrival from behind → name fades in → details/hands
  → the moment → exit.
- *Material* — each chapter makes something the finale uses (paper → confetti; potatoes → pie).
- *Revisit (then and now)* — the place today → dissolve to the old photo/video of it → its own sound → back.
- *Pose loop* (photoshoots) — direct → hands adjust → shutter → camera screen → **the real photo**.
- Plant early → pay off late. Endings rhyme with the opening and land on a small, quiet, candid moment (the youngest,
  the empty lit room, a ritual for two); no big outro speech, no montage recap.

**Sound is the main tool**
- Natural/ambient sound first in each section. Music is **earned or removed**, not a percentage: switched **on** by an
  on-screen action (needle drop, lamp, door, shutter) or once we've settled; switched **off** by a change (nightfall,
  arrival, a confession, tears, chaos). Silence marks chapter breaks.
- Music ≈ 12–16 dB under speech; full only in event/shoot montages; cinematic passages run one music arc (enter on the
  first strong wide, peak, fade on arrival somewhere quiet) and are cut on phrases, not beats.
- A 3–5 s near-silence before a big reveal.
- Music must be real and warm (acoustic, lo-fi, cinematic strings/pads). Our synth-only SoundCraft is the weakest link:
  ask the user for licensed tracks (Artlist / Epidemic / YouTube Audio Library), or build cues from the footage's own
  sounds, or lean on natural sound (one reference has no music at all and 846k views).

**Transitions**
Hard cuts. Physical transitions (an object carried out of frame → empty hold → next place; a window; light changing).
Dissolves only with meaning (model → real place; then → now). Dips to black between big blocks; fade at the end. No
whooshes, flashes, glitches. A HUD/REC frame is allowed **once** as a meaningful register switch (e.g. an outtake after
the credits), never as decoration. Use a **real** flash/strobe from the footage instead of synthetic ones.

**Text — two voices, never labels of the obvious**
- *Quiet voice:* small white serif/humanist subtitles, no box, sentence case, first person, talking to the viewer
  ("How's life recently?", "Come in, darling…"); humble, honest, a little funny ("lol, oops", "*seriously?*"). Narrating
  captions may appear on many shots when there's no voiceover; place/dish names small, bilingual where relevant.
- *Playful voice:* bold yellow caps labels with an arrow that are a joke and a name tag at once ("NO LONGER A
  SOPHOMORE →"); subtitle jokes (translator brackets, keyboard-smash, asterisks); a mock-TV identity for one ritual.
- *Frame-level wit:* feature-film credits over everyday moments ("DIRECTED BY…" over breakfast).
- Never: pills on every scene, captions describing what we can see, watermarks, subscribe cards.

**Look**
Soft, filmic, warm, highlights rolling off (y98 ≈ 72–90). Grade per piece: evening/interior with practicals low-key
(y50 ≈ 15–35); daytime family brighter (y50 ≈ 42–55). Blacks may be crushed or gently lifted, but never HDR-crunchy.
Greens toward olive, warm amber interiors vs cool daylight. Light itself is a subject. Natural, dense, warm skin.

**Camera language to find in footage**
Locked-off wides people walk into → 2–4 hand/object close-ups → a medium; people from behind entering spaces; a
recurring frame (a window, a train, a seat); quiet faces as valued as loud ones; long "talking while living" takes.

**Photoshoot content specifically**
End each look on the photographer's **real photo** (ask the user for the selects), shown full-frame/pillarboxed in
silence ~2–3 s; build up to it with the pose loop; cut from the real strobe to the photo it made; let the couple's
laughter be the reveal; music only in shoot montages; the photographer a character, not the hero; a hard cut into each
new outfit; end on a quiet candid or a slow gallery of the photos in silence.

**Thumbnails & titles**
Thumbnail = a real, filmic frame from the footage with **no text, arrows or emoji**: the single most emotional,
story-telling still (faces 20–45 % of height; quiet or genuine reactions). Title = stakes and specifics: first person,
names the people/relationship, one concrete hook (a number, a duration), CAPS on 1–3 key words, optional `*lowercase
aside*`, curiosity that teases feeling and is paid off. Never repeat the title on the image.

**Length**
No cap — the material decides (the user: "as long as you can, as far as you show all the important and nice
scenes"). References run 7–48 min. Long-form ≠ slow-form — every chapter has a mini-goal and payoff.
