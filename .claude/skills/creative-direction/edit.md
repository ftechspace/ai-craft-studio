# Edit playbook (film-editor, producer)

**This user's direction first** (from 26 reference videos — see `taste-profile`): intimate real-life storytelling.
Pick one **story spine** (clock/light, condition arc, roll call + one chapter per person, countdown + "coming up",
place chapters entered the same way, material spine, then-and-now, photoshoot pose loop → the real photo). Median shot
4–6 s; real moments held 15 s–3 min; natural sound first; music earned (switched on by an on-screen action) and removed
(nightfall, tears, a confession); silence between chapters; physical transitions (object leaves frame → empty hold →
next place); plant → pay off; end small, rhyming with the opening. Text = a person's caption voice, rarely labels.
The techniques below serve that direction.

## What makes an edit feel authored, not assembled
- **A point of view.** Decide whose film it is. Cut *to* reactions from that person; let their sound lead.
- **Story turns.** In long-form, something must change every 30–45 s: a new place, a new problem, a reveal, a joke
  paying off, a shift in music or energy. List the turns before cutting. A 5-minute piece needs ~8–10.
- **Scenes have shape.** Each scene: hook (start late, on action or a line) → development → a button (a laugh, a
  look, a line that lands) → out. Leave every scene slightly early, on the button.
- **Sound leads picture.** Pre-lap the next scene's sound (J-cut) to pull the viewer forward; let a laugh or line
  carry over the cut (L-cut). Use a real sound as a transition (shutter click → cut, strobe pop → flash cut, a door,
  a clap). Silence before a big moment is the strongest "effect" you have.
- **Match cuts with meaning.** Gesture → same gesture (his hand to her hand), shape → shape (softbox circle → face),
  colour → colour (her earth dress → the caramel backdrop), movement direction continuing across outfits/time.
  Find 2–3 real ones in the footage and build transitions around them.
- **The one shot.** Every film has a shot that *is* the film. Find it, protect it (hold it longer, give it silence or
  the music's peak), set it up so it lands.
- **Running gags & callbacks.** Something that happens twice is a pattern; three times is a joke. Plant early,
  pay off late (the photographer's thumbs-up, her laugh into her hand, "one more").
- **Rhythm variety.** Mix long holds with short bursts; a burst only works after holds. Cut on the emotional beat,
  not a grid. Speed changes only on peaks with a reason.
- **Text as voice, not labels.** Captions should add wit, subtext or what the picture can't say ("He's on heel-strap
  duty"), never describe the obvious ("They pose"). Fewer, funnier, better-timed. Section labels only if they add
  rhythm or structure — consider integrating them into the world (a slate, a contact-sheet number, the photographer's
  tablet) instead of floating pills.
- **Open strong, end stronger.** Cold opens tease the best moment or pose a question; endings call back to the
  opening, land on the strongest image, or end on an unexpected button (outtake, wink, one last line).

## Generic tells to avoid
Uniform shot lengths · every scene with a floating label · captions describing the picture · dissolves/flashes as
decoration · music wall-to-wall with no dynamics · the "best" moments all in one montage · ending on a fade with
nothing to land · effects with no motivation.

## Before you cut, write
Truth · POV · the one shot · 3 match cuts found · the running gag/callback · the turns list with times · where the
silence goes · the ending button.
